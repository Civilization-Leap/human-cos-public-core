# Human-COS Public Core — Participation Conduct

Human-COS Public Core exists for independent verification, criticism, falsification and reproducibility. Disagreement is expected and is not a conduct violation.

## Expected conduct

Participants should:

- distinguish evidence, inference, values and uncertainty;
- criticize claims, methods and implementations rather than targeting people;
- preserve material disagreement instead of forcing consensus;
- disclose relevant conflicts of interest when they materially affect a claim;
- avoid publishing private data, credentials or untriaged security details;
- respect legal, professional and safety boundaries;
- make reproducibility claims no stronger than the evidence supports.

## Unacceptable conduct

The following are not acceptable participation methods:

- harassment, threats or targeted abuse;
- doxxing or publication of private personal information;
- fabricated evidence or deliberate misrepresentation of another participant's work;
- attempts to use a security report, exploit or high-risk implementation detail to increase avoidable real-world harm;
- presenting repository merge authority as authority over truth, professional judgment or real-world decisions.

## Enforcement scope

Repository maintainers may moderate repository-hosted participation channels for conduct and safety. That moderation power does not establish superior authority over external research conclusions or independent Human-COS implementations.

Security vulnerabilities must use GitHub Private Vulnerability Reporting rather than public Issues.
